The 27-Point Website Revenue Leak Checklist

This checklist identifies the 27 most common points where potential customers quietly leave your website without making contact. Every item you score is a known conversion barrier, a place where your site is leaking revenue. Score each item honestly: Pass = 2 pts  ·  Partial = 1 pt  ·  Fail = 0 pts. Maximum 54 points. Scores save automatically.

46–54: Optimised
36–45: Functional
24–35: At Risk
12–23: Critical
0–11: Emergency
Optimised (46–54), Your website is performing as a genuine business asset. Focus shifts to analytics, tracking, and incremental testing rather than fixing fundamental gaps.
Functional (36–45), Strong foundations with identifiable gaps. Each "Fail" in this checklist represents missed enquiries that are directly recoverable with targeted improvements.
At Risk (24–35), Your website is leaking revenue every day. The specific gaps you identify here are actively costing you clients, many are fixable within weeks without a full redesign.
Critical (12–23), Major structural issues are suppressing conversions across multiple sections. A professional audit and partial or full rebuild is likely required to turn this around.
Emergency (0–11), The website is actively discouraging potential clients across nearly every touchpoint. Urgent and comprehensive professional attention is required immediately.

Homepage
Items 1–6  ·  max 12 pts
1
Is your value proposition visible within 5 seconds?
Open your homepage on an unfamiliar device and set a 5-second timer. A strong headline names the problem you solve and who you serve.
2
Is there a clear call to action above the fold?
Check both desktop and mobile without scrolling. The CTA must be a visible, contrasting button with an outcome-based label, not "Click Here".
3
Does the headline explain who the service is for?
Read your homepage headline aloud. Could it apply to any business in any industry? Audience-specific headlines convert significantly better.
4
Is there a clear explanation of how you work?
A simple "How It Works" section, even three steps, removes the uncertainty that stops people from making first contact.
5
Does the homepage address the visitor's primary objection?
What is the most common concern from new clients before they commit? Is it directly addressed anywhere on your homepage?
6
Is the navigation simple and logical?
Count your main nav items, more than 7 is generally too many. Ask someone unfamiliar to find your contact page and note how long it takes.

Speed
Items 7–10  ·  max 8 pts
7
Does your homepage load in under 3 seconds?
Visit pagespeed.web.dev and enter your URL. A score above 90 is good; below 50 is urgent and actively costing you visitors before they see anything.
8
Are images compressed and optimised?
Any image over 200KB on a live site is a candidate for compression (TinyPNG). Use WebP format where supported for significant size reduction.
9
Are unused plugins removed?
Every installed plugin loads code on every page visit. Deactivated plugins should be deleted entirely, not just turned off.
10
Is your hosting adequate for your traffic level?
Check GTmetrix Time to First Byte, above 600ms indicates a hosting problem. Target: managed WordPress or VPS with TTFB under 200ms.

Trust
Items 11–15  ·  max 10 pts
11
Are Google reviews visible on the website?
Displaying reviews on-site ensures all visitors see social proof regardless of how they found you. A live widget is more credible than screenshots.
12
Are client logos or partner logos displayed?
A "Trusted By" section with 5–10 recognisable logos significantly increases perceived authority for visitors who don't yet know you.
13
Are testimonials recent and specific?
Testimonials naming a specific problem, solution, and outcome are far more persuasive than generic praise. "Great service!" converts almost no one.
14
Is there a visible location or service area?
A site with no location information feels anonymous. State your base location and service coverage in the footer and on the contact page.
15
Does the website have a valid SSL certificate?
The URL must start with "https://". A "Not Secure" browser warning causes many visitors to leave before reading a single word of content.

User Experience
Items 16–19  ·  max 8 pts
16
Is the website fully functional on mobile?
Open your website on a smartphone. Can you read all text without zooming? No horizontal scroll? All buttons tappable? Over 60% of visitors are on mobile.
17
Can a visitor contact you in under 2 clicks from any page?
Count clicks to your contact form from the homepage, a service page, and a blog post. Your phone number should be in the header or footer.
18
Is there a CTA on every key page?
Visit your homepage, each service page, About, and contact. Does every single page have at least one clear, visible call to action?
19
Is the font size and contrast readable?
Body text should be at least 16px. Check contrast at WebAIM Contrast Checker, minimum 4.5:1 ratio. Light grey on white is not readable.

Conversion
Items 20–23  ·  max 8 pts
20
Is the contact form under 5 fields?
Every additional field reduces completion. Name, email, phone (optional), and message is the ideal maximum. Remove the "How did you hear about us?" field.
21
Is there a confirmation after form submission?
Submit a test enquiry right now. Does a clear confirmation appear immediately? A missing confirmation also breaks conversion tracking in Analytics.
22
Is there a reason to contact you now rather than later?
Without urgency, interested visitors bookmark and never return. "Book a Free 30-Minute Review" consistently outperforms "Contact Us" as a CTA.
23
Is Google Analytics (or equivalent) installed?
Visit analytics.google.com and verify your site is sending data. Check that at least one conversion event (form submission) is configured and tracking.

Technical
Items 24–27  ·  max 8 pts
24
Are there any broken links on the website?
Use Screaming Frog (free) or Google Search Console → Coverage to find 404 errors. Any removed page needs a 301 redirect to the nearest equivalent.
25
Is the website monitored for downtime?
Set up UptimeRobot (free) to alert you if your site goes offline. Without monitoring, your site may be down for hours or days without you knowing.
26
Are all plugins and the CMS platform up to date?
Log in to WordPress → Dashboard → Updates. Outdated software is the single most common entry point for hacks on small business websites.
27
Is there a recent, tested backup?
Ask your host when the last backup was taken and whether it has been restored and tested. Daily off-site backups are the minimum professional standard.

The Website Health Diagnostic Workbook

This diagnostic assesses the underlying health of your website across 10 dimensions: how well it communicates purpose, guides visitors, captures leads, builds trust, loads quickly, works on mobile, stays secure, ranks in search, remains stable, and converts. Unlike the checklist above, this section identifies structural vulnerabilities that may not yet be obvious, but will compound over time. Score each: 2 = fully in place  ·  1 = incomplete  ·  0 = missing. Maximum 100 points.

81–100: Optimised
61–80: Functional
41–60: At Risk
21–40: Critical
0–20: Emergency
Optimised (81–100), Your site is in excellent structural health. The focus shifts to conversion optimisation and ongoing maintenance rather than finding and fixing problems.
Functional (61–80), Well maintained with specific areas to improve. These are growth limiters rather than emergencies, address them in order of lowest section score first.
At Risk (41–60), Multiple underlying issues exist. Speed, security, and SEO problems tend to amplify each other, they are best addressed together rather than piecemeal over time.
Critical (21–40), Significant structural problems are actively limiting your search visibility and eroding visitor trust. A professional remediation plan is strongly advised.
Emergency (0–20), Immediate professional attention is required across most dimensions. This site is very likely costing as much in lost opportunities as it currently earns.
