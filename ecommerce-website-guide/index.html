Chapter 2

The 6 Ways Ecommerce Stores Lose Revenue

Most ecommerce performance issues fall into one of six categories. Knowing the category helps you prioritise fixes with the highest revenue impact.

Storefront Clarity Leaks
Visitors cannot quickly understand what you sell, who it is for, or why your products are different.
Speed Leaks
Slow category/product pages kill add-to-cart rates — especially on mobile where most ecommerce traffic now comes from.
Product Page Leaks
Weak product content, poor imagery, missing specs, and little social proof reduce buyer confidence.
Trust Leaks
Unclear shipping, returns, and payment security signals create hesitation right before purchase.
Checkout Leaks
Too many checkout fields, hidden shipping costs, and forced account creation trigger abandonment.
Retention Leaks
No lifecycle email/SMS follow-up means one-time buyers never become repeat customers.

Chapter 4 — Storefront

27-Point Checklist: Storefront (Items 1–3)

Mark each item Pass (2 pts), Partial (1 pt), or Fail (0 pts). Your section score updates live below.

1
Is your core value proposition clear within 5 seconds?
Open your homepage on mobile. Can a first-time visitor immediately understand what category of products you sell and why they should buy from you? “Premium ergonomic office chairs delivered in 48 hours” is stronger than “Welcome to our store.”
2
Is there a clear path to shop from above the fold?
Users should see “Shop Best Sellers”, “Shop by Category”, or equivalent immediately. If your hero section is only brand storytelling with no clear shopping action, you are delaying the buying journey.
3
Is navigation and on-site search intuitive?
A shopper should find any top-selling product in under 3 clicks. Search should handle common misspellings and return relevant products quickly. Poor discovery kills high-intent traffic.

27-Point Checklist: Storefront (Items 4–6)

4
Are category pages structured to reduce decision fatigue?
Filters (price, size, color, rating), sort options, and clear product grouping are essential. Flat, cluttered category grids force shoppers to leave rather than decide.
5
Are promotions and offers current, clear, and credible?
Outdated banners (“Black Friday 2024”) or vague offers hurt trust. Promotions should include clear terms and expiry information, with obvious relevance to current shoppers.

Is merchandising strategy visible (best sellers, bundles, recommendations)?
High-performing stores guide choice using “Best Sellers”, “Frequently Bought Together”, “Complete the Look”, and seasonal merchandising. If every product is presented equally, none gets momentum.

Chapter 4 — Speed

27-Point Checklist: Speed (Items 7–10)

7
Do product and category pages load in under 3 seconds on mobile?
Use pagespeed.web.dev on your top category and top product pages, not just homepage. Ecommerce bottlenecks often appear deeper in the funnel where dynamic scripts and heavy media load.
8
Are product images and videos fully optimised?
Large images are essential for ecommerce but must be compressed, lazy-loaded, and delivered in modern formats (WebP/AVIF). One unoptimised product gallery can cripple mobile conversion.
9
Is app/plugin bloat under control?
Each ecommerce app/plugin often injects scripts into every page. Audit your stack quarterly. Remove tools that do not directly improve revenue, retention, or operations.
10
Is your hosting/CDN setup suitable for ecommerce traffic spikes?
Promotions, launches, and ad pushes create traffic peaks. If your store slows or crashes during campaigns, infrastructure is a revenue leak. CDN and server-level caching are baseline requirements.

Chapter 4 — Trust

27-Point Checklist: Trust (Items 11–15)

11
Are trust signals visible at key buying moments?
Display review summaries, secure payment icons, and guarantee badges on product pages, cart, and checkout — not just in footer. Trust must appear where purchase anxiety happens.
12
Are shipping, delivery times, and return policy explicit before checkout?
Hidden shipping costs and vague delivery windows are major abandonment triggers. Policies should be plain-English, prominent, and consistent across product/cart/checkout.
13
Are product reviews recent, credible, and specific?
Strong reviews mention real use cases, fit/quality details, and delivery experience. A product page with zero reviews (or only generic reviews) underperforms significantly.
14
Is your business identity visible and credible?
Include company details, ABN (if relevant), support channels, and clear contact methods. Anonymous-looking stores trigger scam concerns and kill conversion.
15
Is checkout clearly secured (HTTPS + secure payment messaging)?
Security messaging during checkout reduces anxiety: padlock, “Secure payment processed by [provider],” and accepted payment options should be obvious before card entry.

Chapter 4 — Shopping UX

27-Point Checklist: Shopping UX (Items 16–19)

16
Is the shopping experience fully functional on mobile?
Can users browse collections, view images, select variants, add to cart, and checkout without friction on a phone? Mobile is the default storefront for most categories.
17
Can users reach cart/checkout in 1 tap from most pages?
Persistent cart visibility and frictionless cart access are critical. Hidden cart icons and unclear progress through checkout hurt completion rates.
18
Are product pages designed for decision-making, not just browsing?
Strong product pages include clear specs, sizing/fit, benefits, FAQs, delivery info, and social proof. If users must leave to find answers, conversion drops.
19
Is readability and tap-target accessibility compliant?
16px+ body text, strong contrast, and 44×44px tap targets should be default. Accessibility is both conversion and compliance insurance for ecommerce.

Chapter 4 — Checkout & Conversion

27-Point Checklist: Conversion (Items 20–23)

20
Is checkout short, simple, and guest-friendly?
Forced account creation and excessive fields reduce completion. Offer guest checkout and collect only what is necessary to fulfil and support the order.
21
Are total costs transparent before payment?
Unexpected shipping/taxes at final step are one of the top abandonment causes. Estimated delivery and full cost should be visible early in checkout flow.
22
Are preferred payment methods available and visible?
Depending on market, methods like Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal, Buy Now Pay Later, and local options can materially improve conversion. Remove payment friction where buyers are ready to commit.
23
Are analytics events and cart recovery flows set up correctly?
You should track view_item, add_to_cart, begin_checkout, purchase, and abandoned cart recovery performance. Without event-level visibility, optimisation decisions are guesswork.

Chapter 4 — Technical

27-Point Checklist: Technical (Items 24–27)

24
Are there no critical broken links or dead product pages?
Run Screaming Frog or Search Console coverage checks. Broken product URLs should redirect to relevant replacements, not generic homepages.
25
Is uptime and checkout error monitoring in place?
Monitor both storefront availability and checkout transaction failures. A store that “looks online” but fails at payment is silently losing high-intent buyers.
26
Are platform, theme, and extensions consistently updated?
Outdated components are both a performance and security liability in ecommerce. Update cadence should be managed with staging/testing, not ad hoc emergency fixes.
27
Is there a recent, tested backup including orders and customer data?
A backup that excludes orders/customers or has never been tested is not sufficient for ecommerce. Validate full-store recovery at least quarterly.

Chapter 8

When You Need a Professional

Some ecommerce issues look like simple tweaks but are actually structural. These red flags indicate professional support is the smartest path.

  • Checkout conversion remains poor after basic fixes — likely deeper UX, trust, or payment-friction issues
  • Theme/app stack is unstable or heavily customised — making changes without architecture review can break core flows
  • Core Web Vitals remain poor despite optimisation — often indicates architectural or hosting bottlenecks
  • Recurring inventory sync/order sync errors — integration-level issues require specialist implementation
  • No reliable analytics event tracking — impossible to optimise what you cannot measure
  • Security/compliance uncertainty around payment handling — must be resolved professionally, not guessed
  • Conversion dependent on paid ads, with weak organic and repeat revenue — retention/lifecycle strategy gap
Rule of thumb

If you fail more than three technical/checkout items and cannot identify root causes within one week, book a professional ecommerce audit before spending more on traffic.

Chapter 10

The 90-Day Ecommerce Revenue Recovery Roadmap

Use this roadmap to convert audit insights into measurable ecommerce outcomes.

Month 1 — Quick Wins & Leak Sealing
Fix trust and clarity leaks first: improve value proposition, shipping&returns messaging, product page essentials, and checkout transparency. Compress media and remove app/plugin bloat. These are often high-impact changes with low implementation complexity.
Month 2 — Structural Conversion Improvements
Address deeper UX and technical issues: checkout simplification, mobile flow optimisation, category architecture refinement, and reliable event tracking. If needed, engage specialists for platform performance and conversion architecture.
Month 3 — Optimisation & Retention Loops
Run controlled experiments (A/B tests), analyse funnel drop-off points, and improve repeat-purchase systems (post-purchase email/SMS, reorder journeys, loyalty incentives). Build systems that compound customer lifetime value.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

I am not technical — can I still use this guide?

Yes. The framework is written in plain English. You can identify leaks and prioritise work even if you never touch code directly.

What if my score is low?

A low score is useful — it gives you a clear roadmap for measurable improvements. Focus on quick wins first, then strategic fixes.

Do I need to hire an agency to fix everything?

No. Many clarity/trust/content improvements are DIY. Use professional help where architecture, checkout, performance, or integration risks are high.

How long does full completion take?

Allow 90–120 minutes for both parts, including competitor benchmarking and initial action plan.

How often should we repeat this diagnostic?

Quarterly for growth-stage stores; at minimum twice per year for stable stores, and after major platform/theme/app changes.

What should I do right after finishing?

Prioritise your top three leaks, assign owners, and schedule implementation immediately. Delay is the most expensive decision in ecommerce optimisation.

The 7 Most Common Ecommerce Traps & DIY vs. Developer

Trap 1 — App/plugin overload

Too many overlapping tools create script conflicts, slow pages, and difficult troubleshooting.

Trap 2 — No event tracking strategy

Stores often run ads without reliable funnel data, leading to spend decisions based on incomplete attribution.

Trap 3 — Checkout treated as “set and forget”

Checkout must be measured and optimised continuously. Small friction points create major abandonment at scale.

Trap 4 — No staging workflow

Editing live store directly increases risk of broken checkout or inventory logic during trading hours.

Trap 5 — Retention ignored

Focusing only on acquisition while neglecting post-purchase lifecycle erodes margin and increases CAC pressure.

Trap 6 — Data ownership ambiguity

Ensure you control analytics, ad accounts, pixel access, email platform, and store credentials.

Trap 7 — Choosing on price alone

Cheap builds often become expensive through rework, downtime, and lost conversion opportunities.
